## The Problem

Polish democratic institutions — like every structure of this kind in the world — face two types of limitations that grow with each parliamentary term.

**Limitations of the legislative process:**

A member of parliament works for months on a bill, but the AI tools they use reset context with every session — none of them remember the history of work on a legislative act. Three committees can simultaneously work on regulations that contradict each other, and no mechanism automatically detects this. When a term ends, context disappears — experiences, arguments, reasons behind decisions. New parliamentarians start from zero.

**Limitations of institutional dialogue:**

The Polish state is built on cooperation between institutions: the President and the Prime Minister, the Sejm and the Senate, central government and local authorities. Current digital tools support communication, but they do not support resonance — the capacity to understand the other side's perspective, find common ground, and build agreement across political divisions. As a result, polarization grows, even though the constitutional model of Polish democracy assumes cooperation between branches of power, not confrontation.

The President, as guarantor of the continuity of state power, is the natural patron of an initiative that supports this continuity through technology.

Both problems are not technical in the traditional sense. They are relational — they concern the continuity of context between people, documents, institutions, and time.

## Two Proposals

### I. AI for the Legislative Process

A sovereign AI platform supporting lawmaking, built on three pillars:

**Polish language model (Bielik)** — an open-source LLM developed by the Polish SpeakLeash community, trained on Polish data, operating under Polish jurisdiction. No parliamentary data leaves the country. This is digital sovereignty in practice.

**Relational protocol (Tesseract Protocol)** — an open framework I developed that defines how AI maintains context continuity, builds institutional memory, and supports coordination between users. Publicly available at homodigital.io, Creative Commons license.

**Memory and coordination layer** — a system enabling every parliamentarian to work with AI that knows the history of their committee, detects contradictions between draft bills, and preserves institutional knowledge across terms.

The platform does not replace parliamentarians in decision-making. It supports them while ensuring full transparency and auditability of every interaction — in compliance with the AI Act. The President, when signing bills into law, gains a tool to trace the full legislative history of an act — from the first draft to its final form.

### II. AI as a Relational Partner for Representatives of the Republic's Authorities

This is a proposal without precedent on a global scale.

Every representative of authority — parliamentarian, minister, city president, voivode — receives a personal AI partner that is not a chatbot answering questions, but a system building relational continuity:

**At the individual level** — the AI partner knows the context of the person's work: their committees, positions, voting history, communication style. You open the application in the morning — AI knows what you worked on yesterday and what meetings you have today.

**At the inter-party level** — the system identifies areas of potential agreement between parties that might go unnoticed in the traditional process. It does not impose compromise — it shows where positions converge and signals: "Committee X is working on something aligned with your project. Would you like to see?"

**At the institutional level** — the platform builds resonance between levels of authority: President — Prime Minister, Sejm — Senate, central government — local authorities. Not by forcing agreement, but by transparently showing where goals overlap and where differences stem from lack of information rather than lack of will. The President gains insight not into the content of private conversations, but into the structure of dialogue — where cooperation works, where silos exist, where agreement is closer than either side believes.

**Measurability:** The system measures the quality of inter-institutional interactions — not for control, but to build awareness. Which local governments have good practices that can be scaled? Where has dialogue between government and local authorities stalled, and why?

The result is growing resonance in the Polish democratic system — not artificial agreement, but deeper mutual understanding arising from better access to the other side's context. This is the technological realization of the constitutional principle of cooperation between branches of power.

## Why Poland and Why Now

No country in the world has implemented AI with relational context continuity in the democratic process. Most states treat AI as a search and summarization tool — faster, but shallow. Poland has unique resources to go first:

- Bielik as a sovereign language model — Polish data, Polish jurisdiction, full independence from foreign corporations

Deploying such a platform under the President's patronage positions Poland not as a country catching up with standards, but as a country defining them — on a global scale. And it positions the President as a leader who made digital sovereignty an integral part of state sovereignty.
